**If you're booking an MSC cruise from Southampton, one of the biggest questions you'll probably have is whether it's worth paying extra for a drinks package.**

It's something I get asked regularly by customers, and my answer is always pretty much the same. It depends on what you drink, how much you drink and, perhaps most importantly, who you're travelling with.

I've sailed with MSC myself, I've experienced the drinks packages firsthand, and I've helped plenty of customers work out whether they're better off booking with drinks included or paying for them individually once onboard.

Personally, I wouldn't want to cruise without a drinks package. But that doesn't necessarily mean I'd recommend one to everyone.

So, let's have a proper look at what you get, what drinks actually cost onboard and whether MSC's Premium Extra package represents good value for money.

## How Much Does MSC Premium Extra Cost?

One of the things I always recommend when booking an MSC cruise is comparing the price with and without drinks included.

At the time of writing, I'm able to quote Premium Extra from **£28 per person, per day** when booked as part of certain cruise fares from Southampton.

To put that into perspective, here's what that works out to:

| Cruise duration | Per person | Two adults |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3 nights | £84 | £168 |
| 5 nights | £140 | £280 |
| 7 nights | £196 | £392 |
| 14 nights | £392 | £784 |

*These examples are based on £28 per person, per night. Prices vary depending on the sailing, offer and booking conditions.*

Now, £392 for two adults on a seven-night cruise might sound like quite a lot of money, particularly when you've already paid for the holiday itself.

But when you start looking at the actual prices of drinks onboard, you might be surprised how quickly that amount can add up.

And remember, it's not just alcoholic drinks that count.

## What's Included in MSC's Premium Extra Drinks Package?

Premium Extra is MSC's top-tier drinks package, offering a much wider selection than the more basic packages.

It includes a selection of:

- Premium-brand spirits, including gin, vodka, whisky and rum
- Cocktails and mixed drinks made with premium spirits
- Wines and sparkling wines by the glass
- Draught and bottled beers
- Soft drinks and fruit juices
- Speciality coffees, teas and hot drinks
- Mocktails and other non-alcoholic options
- Still and sparkling water, subject to the arrangements onboard

There's a pretty extensive selection, and personally I've never felt particularly restricted by what I could order.

There are, however, a few things worth knowing.

MSC currently limits alcoholic drinks purchased through the package to 15 per person, per day. That certainly doesn't mean you need to drink anywhere near that amount to get value for money!

There are also exclusions, including minibars, room service and certain other items. Availability can vary between venues and ships, so it's always worth checking the current terms.

One thing I particularly like about Premium Extra is the flexibility. Whether you're having a coffee in the morning, a drink around the pool in the afternoon or a cocktail after dinner, you're not constantly thinking about how much each drink is costing you.

For me, that's a big part of being on holiday.

## Why I Personally Always Choose a Drinks Package

I've spent plenty of time aboard MSC Virtuosa, including our honeymoon cruise from Southampton, and I know exactly how I like to enjoy my time onboard.

I'm quite partial to a gin and tonic in the evening. Usually two or three, and I'll happily work my way through a few of the different gin cocktails on the menu as well.

But it's not all about the alcohol.

One of my favourite things about being on a cruise is getting up early, grabbing a nice coffee and heading outside to watch the sunrise.

There's something about sitting on a balcony, or heading to one of the decks at the back of the ship, with a coffee in hand and nothing but the sea around you.

It's those little moments that make cruising special for me.

Throughout the day I'll probably have another coffee, a couple of soft drinks, perhaps some water and then a few alcoholic drinks in the evening.

Individually, none of that seems particularly excessive. But when you start adding up what those drinks would cost over a week or two, you can quickly see why having a package makes sense.

I also like knowing I've already paid for it before I step onboard.

I don't want to spend my holiday looking at a drinks menu and deciding whether I really want to spend another £10 or £14 on something.

If I fancy it, I'll order it.

**For me, that's what makes a drinks package worthwhile. It's as much about convenience as it is about saving money.**

And we've not even factored in coffees, soft drinks or anything else you might order throughout the day.

## Let's Look at a Typical Day Onboard

Rather than just looking at the cost of individual drinks, I think the best way to work out whether a package is worthwhile is to look at what you might realistically consume throughout a normal day.

Let's use the prices from that Virtuosa menu.

Imagine you have:

| Drink | Total |
|---|---:|
| 2 cappuccinos | £8.00 |
| 2 soft drinks | £7.00 |
| 1 litre of water | £3.25 |
| 2 premium cocktails | £28.00 |
| **Total** | **£46.25** |

That's £46.25 in drinks over the course of a day, without going particularly overboard.

Compare that with £28 for Premium Extra, and that's a potential difference of £18.25 for the day.

Over seven nights, that works out to £127.75.

Now, that's only an illustration based on an older menu, and you'd need to check whether your specific choices are included in the package.

But it highlights why I think drinks packages can offer such good value, particularly for people who enjoy cocktails or premium spirits.

On the other hand, remove the two cocktails from that example and your total drops to £18.25.

Suddenly, paying £28 for the package doesn't look quite so attractive.

And that's exactly why I don't believe there's one right answer for everybody.

## What If One Person Doesn't Drink Alcohol?

This is probably one of the most important things to consider, and something I always discuss with customers.

MSC generally requires everyone sharing the same cabin to purchase the appropriate drinks package. For two adults, that normally means both need Premium Extra if one person wants it.

Children would have the appropriate Minors Package instead.

So, let's imagine you've got a couple travelling together.

One enjoys a few beers or cocktails throughout the day, while the other doesn't drink alcohol at all.

At £28 per adult, you're paying £56 per day between you.

That's £392 over a seven-night cruise.

Now, the person who doesn't drink alcohol might still enjoy several coffees, soft drinks, mocktails and other drinks throughout the day.

If that's the case, they could still get reasonable value from the package.

But what if they only drink a couple of coffees and perhaps one soft drink?

Suddenly, you're relying on the person drinking alcohol to consume enough to justify most of that £56 daily cost.

And that's where I'd start looking much more closely at the numbers.

**My advice would be to work out what you'd both spend, rather than just looking at what the person drinking alcohol would save.**

Sometimes you'll be better off without the package, and there's absolutely nothing wrong with that.

## Don't Forget About Sea Days and Port Days

Here's something else I think is worth considering when deciding whether to add drinks.

Look at your itinerary.

If you're doing a seven-night cruise with several sea days, chances are you'll be spending a fair amount of time onboard enjoying the ship.

You might have a coffee after breakfast, a drink by the pool, something with lunch, a few drinks in the evening and perhaps a cocktail while watching a show.

On those days, getting value from Premium Extra can be relatively straightforward.

But what about a port-intensive itinerary where you're off the ship exploring for eight or nine hours most days?

You might only have a coffee before leaving and a couple of drinks when you return.

Your drinks spending could be quite different.

Obviously, everyone's holiday habits are different, but it's worth thinking about how much time you'll actually spend onboard.

And remember, the package is purchased for the cruise, not just the individual days you think you'll use it most.

## Is It Cheaper to Book Drinks Before Your Cruise?

Generally speaking, I would always recommend checking the cost of adding drinks when you first book your cruise.

MSC regularly runs promotions where Premium Extra can be included as part of the fare at an attractive rate.

And sometimes the difference between the cruise-only fare and the drinks-inclusive fare is much smaller than people expect.

That's why when I'm quoting customers, I like to look at the options available rather than automatically assuming one is better than the other.

It's also worth remembering that the prices MSC offers can change, and the package available through a booking promotion isn't necessarily going to cost the same as adding it later.

If you know you're going to want drinks included, it makes sense to compare the options from the beginning.

## So, Is MSC Premium Extra Actually Worth It?

For me personally? Absolutely.

I enjoy my coffees, I enjoy a decent gin and tonic, and I like being able to try different cocktails without having to worry about the cost of each one.

But would I tell every single customer to buy it?

Definitely not.

If you enjoy a few alcoholic drinks, premium cocktails and coffees throughout the day, I think there's a good chance you'll get your money's worth, particularly if you can secure one of the more competitive drinks-inclusive fares.

If you're someone who rarely drinks alcohol, spends most of your days ashore or only has the occasional drink with dinner, paying as you go could work out cheaper.

And if you're travelling as a couple where only one person drinks, I'd definitely recommend comparing the combined cost before making a decision.

Ultimately, a drinks package should make your holiday more enjoyable, not leave you feeling like you need to drink a certain amount every day just to justify the expense.

That's completely the wrong way to look at it.

## My Final Advice Before Booking Your MSC Cruise

Having sailed with MSC myself and spent a lot of time helping customers choose their cruises, my biggest piece of advice is simple.

**Don't just look at the headline cruise price. Look at what you're actually getting for your money.**

Two cruises that appear to be priced similarly can work out quite differently once you start adding things like drinks, Wi-Fi and parking.

And sometimes paying a little more upfront can make a big difference to your overall holiday budget.

Whenever I'm helping somebody book an MSC cruise, I'm more than happy to compare the different fares and explain exactly what you're getting.

If a drinks package makes sense for you, brilliant.

If it doesn't, I'll happily tell you that too.

I'd much rather help you book the right holiday than sell you something you don't really need.
